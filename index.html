Sleep above the treeline.

Ursa is a stone refuge at 1,842 metres in the Făgăraș Mountains: twelve bunks, a wood stove, dinner at seven and a sky with no town lights in it. The only way in is a four-hour walk.

Four hours from the road

Follow the red stripe from the forestry barrier at Poiana Neagră: 7.4 km and 1,202 m of climbing, with water on the way and a stove at the end.

  1. 640 m

    Poiana Neagră

    Start. Parking for 20 cars.

    Leave the car at the forestry barrier. The red stripe starts on the first beech on your left and doesn’t stop until our door. There’s a bus from Victoria to the barrier at 7:40 on weekends.

  2. 1,120 m

    Făgetul Mare

    2.3 km, about 1 h 10 from the barrier.

    An hour of beech forest along Valea Ursului. Fill your bottle at the wooden trough: it’s spring water and the last tap before the refuge.

  3. 1,580 m

    Stâna din Deal

    5.1 km, about 2 h 40 from the barrier.

    The trees give up and the sheepfold appears. From July to September the shepherds sell fresh cheese. Their dogs are loud but they stay with the flock: walk wide, don’t run, and they’ll lose interest.

  4. 1,842 m

    Refugiul Ursa

    7.4 km, about 3 h 45 from the barrier.

    The last climb is short and steep. Take your boots off at the door; there’s tea on the stove and a bunk with your name on it.

Stone outside, pine inside

Shepherds built the first walls here in 1936. We rebuilt the refuge stone by stone in 2019, with sixty-centimetre walls, a pine interior and one stove that heats the whole house.

  • Common room

    A table for sixteen, a cast-iron stove, board games, and the logbook every guest since 2019 has signed.

  • Ursa Mare, 8 bunks

    Four double-deckers with curtains and a reading light on every bunk. The east window gets the sunrise.

  • Ursa Mică, 4 bunks

    For families and small groups. Book all four if you want the door to yourselves.

  • Kitchen

    The warden’s domain. Dinner at 19:00, breakfast from 7:00, tea whenever the kettle is on, which is always.

  • Drying room

    Hooks and a boot rack along the stove flue, so wet gear is dry by breakfast.

  • Porch

    Benches facing south down the valley. The best seat for sunset, unless you’re on the summit.

Dinner and breakfast are included. Vegetarian on request; tell us about allergies when you book.
Water
A spring 40 metres from the door, filtered at the tap inside.
Power
Solar. Enough for lights and charging phones, not for hair dryers.
Signal
None at the refuge. One bar on the ridge, five minutes up. The warden has a satellite phone for emergencies.

Two ways in, one way higher

Every route here is waymarked with a painted symbol on rocks and trees, every 50 to 100 metres. Follow the symbol, not your phone.

Up here the stars go all the way down to the horizon

There’s no road, village or streetlight for nineteen kilometres. On a clear, moonless night the Milky Way runs from the ridge to the valley, and you can find the Plough without looking for it. The warden keeps binoculars and a star chart by the door.

Before you walk up

What about bears?

They live here and we’re the guests. Walk in daylight, talk as you go, and keep food inside your pack or the refuge. The bins are bear-proof, and the warden will tell you if one has been seen on your route that week.

What happens if the weather turns?

Nobody leaves in a storm. If the warden says the ridge isn’t safe, you stay another night and it’s on us.

Can I bring my dog?

Yes, on a lead, on the red-stripe route. Dogs sleep in the entry, not in the bunk rooms, and there’s a blanket for them. Keep them close at the sheepfold.

Do I need a sleeping bag?

No. Every bunk has a duvet and a pillow. Bring a sleeping-bag liner, or rent one for 10 lei.

Can I get there in winter?

Only with a mountain guide and a group booking. The valley route is avalanche terrain from December to April, so email us first and we’ll put you in touch with a guide from Victoria.
